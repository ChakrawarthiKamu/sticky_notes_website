Productivity, 4 min read

How to run a kanban board with sticky notes

Three columns are enough to start: to do, doing and done. Give each column a header note in its own colour, then write one task per note in as few words as you can.

The rule that makes a kanban board work is a limit on the middle column. If "doing" may only hold three notes, you have to finish something before you start something. Moving a note to "done" is the reward.

Use Standard squares for tasks and a Large note for the goal of the week at the top. Clear the "done" column every Friday and count what moved.

Organise, 3 min read

Colour-coding that actually works

Colour systems fail when there are too many colours. Pick four at most and give each one meaning that will not change: for example yellow for tasks, pink for people, mint for ideas, blue for dates.

Write the key on one note and stick it at the corner of your board or the first page of your notebook. After a week you will stop needing it.

A multi-colour set keeps the shades consistent, which matters more than it sounds: two slightly different yellows read as two categories.

Study, 5 min read

Revision with page flags and clear notes

Flag first, write later. On a first read, mark every page you will need again with a page flag and move on. One colour per topic makes the edge of the book into an index.

On the second pass, lay a clear note over diagrams and tables and annotate on the film. The page underneath stays clean, which matters for library books and resale.

Before an exam, pull the clear notes off and line them up on a sheet of paper. That sheet is your summary.

Planning, 3 min read

Plan a whole week on one sticky note

A week-view pad gives each day a narrow column. That is the point: there is room for three things per day, not thirteen.

Fill it in on Sunday evening with the items that have a fixed day. Leave at least one column nearly empty. Stick the sheet on the inside cover of your notebook or the edge of your monitor.

Pair it with a to-do pad for the day itself. The week note tells you what matters, the daily list tells you what is next.

Workspace, 2 min read

The five-minute desk reset

At the end of the day, read every note on your desk once. Anything finished goes in the recycling. Anything still true gets re-stuck in one row along the bottom of your monitor.

Write tomorrow's first task on a fresh note and place it on your keyboard. When you sit down, you start with a decision already made.

It takes five minutes and it is the difference between a wall of notes you read and a wall you have stopped seeing.

Gifting, 3 min read

Sticky notes as gifts: ideas under ₹600

Stationery is one of the few gifts that gets used up, which is exactly why people like receiving it. A boxed set of pads suits students, new joiners and anyone who has just moved desks.

For something smaller, pair shaped notes with a pen and a short message written on the top sheet. For children, round notes and hearts make good reward stickers that Notera off furniture.
